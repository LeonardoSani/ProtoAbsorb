\begin{abstract}
We study a concrete open-world deployment failure: when test batches mix corrupted in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, entropy-minimizing TTA sharpens predictions on \emph{all} inputs---including OOD inputs whose uncertainty should be preserved---creating a direct conflict with OOD detection.
We introduce a \emph{paired contamination protocol} that isolates this conflict, pairing an ID-only oracle against realistic mixed-batch adaptation on identical test streams under a \emph{frozen-detector evaluation contract}---measuring compatibility with a pre-deployed detector that is not co-maintained during adaptation, the relevant contract for systems where the detector was calibrated before deployment.
Across 4 OOD datasets, 2 TTA methods (TENT, ETA), 4 ID fractions, and 5 OOD detectors (MSP, Energy, Mahalanobis, KNN, ViM), the oracle consistently outperforms the realistic baseline for confidence-based and KNN-based detectors (27/32 cells, $p < 0.05$ BH-corrected), with mixed-batch adaptation forfeiting up to $2.89\times$ the gradient-clean OOD-detection benefit in our tested settings (absolute gap up to $+0.345$).
A four-condition mechanistic decomposition identifies \emph{OOD gradient contamination} as the dominant driver (+120--456\% of the total gap), while BatchNorm exposure to OOD inputs without the gradient is actively beneficial in all tested conditions.
We replicate at ImageNet scale (8/8 cells, ResNet-50, NINCO) and in sequential 100-batch streams including non-stationary contamination (8/8 and 16/16 cells).
A cross-method audit across six families (No TTA, TENT, ETA, UniEnt+, SAR, CoTTA) reveals that in our heavy-contamination setting ($\alpha{=}0.9$), ETA ranks first by closed-set accuracy but last by mixed-stream AUROC among entropy-minimizing methods, while CoTTA and SAR substantially narrow the gap---demonstrating that the protocol captures a differentiable safety property.
We recommend reporting the paired contamination gap alongside ID accuracy for TTA work targeting open-world deployment.
\keywords{test-time adaptation \and out-of-distribution detection \and open-world evaluation \and entropy minimization \and paired protocol}
\end{abstract}
